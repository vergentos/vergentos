### Introduction

Are you protective of your website security? Uncertain about what preventative measures you can take to stop hackers from taking down your website or accessing your supposedly secure files and data?

Website security isn't optional—it's essential. Below are practical tips to reduce the risk of your sensitive data getting hacked or stolen.

### 1. Password Security

Many people and business owners use passwords that are memorable to them. We've seen scenarios where:

- People use their first name as the username and surname as the password
- They use their full name as the username and date of birth as the password
- Most commonly, people use their email address as the username and a simple one-word password

All of these approaches are dangerously insecure.

#### How to Create a Strong, Secure Password

First, create a password using various character types:

- **UPPERCASE LETTERS**
- **lowercase letters**
- **Num83r5** (numbers)
- **Symb@ls** (symbols)
- **Punct,ation** (punctuation)

Why? Because hackers use automated tools to scan combinations, typically starting with standard lowercase letters. Using all available character types makes your password exponentially harder to crack. The number of possible combinations becomes astronomical.

Second, choose a password that is **at least 15 characters in length**. Password generator tools can help if you find it challenging to create complex passwords. Mac users can use the built-in Keychain password generator, and browser-based password managers like 1Password or Bitwarden generate and store strong passwords securely.

#### Use a Password Manager

Remembering dozens of complex, unique passwords is impossible. A password manager solves this—you remember one master password, and it handles the rest. Never reuse passwords across sites.

#### Enable Two-Factor Authentication

Wherever possible, enable 2FA. Even if someone obtains your password, they can't access your account without the second factor—typically a code from your phone.

### 2. Software Updates

Whether you're a PC user, use a server for stored files, or have a website, keeping software updated is critical. Outdated software is vulnerable to hackers. In simple terms, running old software is like leaving your front door wide open.

#### Why Updates Matter

All updates released by software companies include the latest security patches. Significant time and investment goes into identifying and fixing vulnerabilities. When you skip updates, you're ignoring fixes for known security holes that hackers actively exploit.

#### Website Plugins and Add-ons

If you have plugins on your website that aren't up to date, they need to be updated or removed immediately. Outdated plugins are one of the easiest ways for hackers to embed malicious code that can steal your data.

For WordPress sites, this is particularly important. A single vulnerable plugin can compromise your entire site. Remove any plugins you're not actively using.

### 3. Be Alert to Suspicious Emails

Hackers have become sophisticated with phishing emails. You must be extremely careful about which emails you open and which links you click.

#### How Phishing Works

Hackers often target your email account first—this is why strong passwords and 2FA are essential. Once they access your email, they send malicious links to all your contacts, spreading the attack.

#### What Happens If You Click

Never click links that look suspicious. If you do, malware may attach itself to your computer. These attacks come in many forms. One of the most common is a keylogger—software that records everything you type and sends it to hackers. You won't know it's there while it quietly captures your bank details, passwords, and sensitive data.

#### How to Spot Phishing Emails

Always check the actual sender address, not just the display name. Click on the sender field to reveal the real email address. It's usually gibberish or a strange name @gmail.com or @hotmail.com. Hackers use free email accounts because they're untraceable.

Red flags to watch for:

- Urgent language demanding immediate action
- Generic greetings like "Dear Customer"
- Spelling and grammar errors
- Links that don't match the supposed sender
- Requests for passwords or personal information

### 4. Use Monitoring Tools

Proactive monitoring helps you catch compromises early. The sooner you discover a problem, the sooner you can fix it.

#### Google Search Console

Sign up for Google Search Console if you haven't already. It's Google's way of communicating issues on your site, including if they detect hacked content. You'll receive alerts when something's wrong.

#### Google Alerts

Set up Google Alerts to notify you of questionable results. For example, if you run a pet accessories site called example.com, create an alert for [site:example.com cheap software]. If hacked content about cheap software appears on your site, you'll know immediately.

Set up multiple alerts for common spam terms hackers inject—pharmaceuticals, gambling, adult content, cheap software. If you're unsure what terms to use, search Google for common spam keywords.

#### Uptime Monitoring

Use services like UptimeRobot or Pingdom to alert you if your site goes down. Sometimes a hack manifests as your site becoming unavailable.

### 5. Regular Backups

Backups won't prevent hacking, but they're essential for recovery. If your site is compromised, a clean backup lets you restore quickly rather than rebuilding from scratch.

#### Backup Best Practices

- Back up daily at minimum
- Store backups off-site (not on the same server)
- Test backups periodically to ensure they work
- Keep multiple backup versions (hackers may have been present for weeks before detection)

### 6. Use HTTPS Everywhere

Your entire website should use HTTPS, not just checkout pages. HTTPS encrypts data between your visitors and your server, preventing interception.

Google also uses HTTPS as a ranking signal, so there's an SEO benefit too. Most hosting providers offer free SSL certificates through Let's Encrypt.

### 7. Limit Login Attempts

Brute force attacks try thousands of password combinations automatically. Limiting login attempts—locking accounts after 5 failed tries, for example—stops these attacks.

For WordPress, plugins like Wordfence or Limit Login Attempts Reloaded provide this protection.

### Conclusion

Website security requires ongoing attention, not a one-time setup. Hackers constantly find new vulnerabilities, and your defences must evolve accordingly.

The basics—strong passwords, updated software, phishing awareness, and monitoring—prevent the vast majority of attacks. Implement these practices today.
