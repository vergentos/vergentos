### It's Funny, Isn't It?

I started working on what would become SEO in 1999. It wasn't called SEO back then. I was active in forums in the mid-90s when the internet first arrived, talking with other tech people about what this thing could actually become.

I remember one conversation clearly. Someone asked: "How does this make money?"

My answer: "Advertising. Build a database of all human knowledge, make it searchable, and sell ads around the results."

Shortly after those conversations, Google was born with advertising as its core business model.

I don't know if anyone from Google was in those forums. I can't prove I planted a seed. Maybe it was pure coincidence. Maybe great minds think alike. Story of my life, really—I saw it coming but didn't capitalise on it.

But I do know this: I saw exactly what was coming.

### The Master Stroke

There's always been smoke and mirrors. But this was genius.

The idea was to create a database of knowledge that was certifiable. If you look at the whole ecosystem in 2025, you'll understand how ingenious it was—because it worked.

For 25 years, the entire world of business—every ounce of human knowledge—populated Google's database. Through articles. Through conversations online. Through podcasts. Through every bit of digital media.

It all got sucked in.

We all helped build it. For free. Every blog post you wrote. Every "SEO-optimised" piece of content. Every carefully crafted meta description. You were training the system.

Ultimately, it would lead to intelligence that would revolutionise the world as we know it.

Welcome to the era of AI.

And here you all are, still talking about how to optimise your site for Google.

### The Database is Complete

Let me be clear about what's happening.

Google spent 25 years building the world's largest knowledge repository, and you all helped them do it for free. Every blog post, every product description, every "optimised" piece of content you created—you were training the system.

The game was: feed the database, rank in the database, get traffic from the database.

That game is over.

### What You Need to Understand

AI doesn't need your website. It doesn't need your carefully crafted meta descriptions. It doesn't need your keyword-optimised H1 tags.

AI consumed all of that already. It learned from it. It became it.

When someone asks ChatGPT or Claude a question, they don't click through to your site. They get the answer directly.

When someone uses Perplexity to research a purchase, they don't visit your product pages. They get a synthesised recommendation from across 50 sources, and your site is just one data point among many.

You're optimising for a system that's being replaced as we speak.

### So What DO You Need to Do?

You need to stop thinking like a publisher and start thinking like an intelligence provider.

The future isn't about ranking #1 for "best CRM software"—it's about your business intelligence being embedded in every AI that answers questions about CRM software. It's about being the SOURCE that AI cites, not the destination it sends traffic to.

The companies that win in the next 5 years won't be the ones with the best SEO. They'll be the ones whose intelligence is so valuable that AI systems can't help but reference them. They'll be the ones creating original research, accumulating proprietary insights, and building knowledge that can't be scraped or replicated.

Think about it: Why would AI send someone to read 2,000 words on your blog when it can synthesise that information into 200 words instantly?

It won't.

It'll only send traffic when you have something AI can't provide—unique data, tools, transactions, or experiences that require human interaction.

### The New Optimisation Game

It's no longer: "How do I get Google to rank me?"

It's: "How do I become so valuable that AI systems have no choice but to know about me, cite me, and recommend me?"

Most of you are still playing draughts while the board has already switched to chess.

I've been watching this shift for 25 years. I saw it coming in those early forum conversations. I watched as everyone rushed to feed the machine. And now I'm watching as everyone frantically tries to optimise for a system that's already obsolete.

### The Beautiful Irony

You spent years learning to game Google's algorithm, and now that algorithm has learned so much that it doesn't need you anymore. The student became the teacher. The database became intelligent. And optimisation became irrelevant.

### My Advice

Stop asking, "How do I rank #1?"

Start asking, "What unique intelligence does my business create that AI needs to know?"

Because if AI doesn't need to know it, neither does anyone else.

The future belongs to intelligence creators, not content optimisers.

Think about that as you adjust your title tags.
